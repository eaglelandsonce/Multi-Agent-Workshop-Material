STEMS Initiative - Build an Agentic Project Team (Next Level)

Scenario Input Paragraph (For Testing Your Skill) A project team must produce and distribute 667 classroom STEM kits reaching approximately 20,000 students (30 students per kit) after their budget was slashed from $2,000 to $500, eliminating the option to purchase automated production machinery. To overcome these severe financial and operational constraints, the team must source ultra-low-cost raw materials (such as brown bags, printer paper, and 10,000 straws), compare supplier pricing and quality tradeoffs, and manually cut, fold, count, and assemble all components. Because there is no labor budget, production relies entirely on recruiting and motivating student volunteers and service groups through social assembly events, while distribution requires evaluating and partnering with an external organization (like a free teacher store) to avoid the administrative bottleneck of managing individual school deliveries. Concurrently, the team is using AI to draft K–12 and NGSS standards-aligned lesson plans, bilingual Social-Emotional Learning (SEL) resources, and educator tools as the initiative scales past 60,000 items across three states. This initiative requires decomposing workstreams into a human-in-the-loop multi-agent system where specialized AI agents handle procurement analysis, schedule and risk mitigation, educational content drafting and quality checks, and volunteer/distribution coordination, while a central orchestrator synthesizes recommendations and escalates all final budget approvals, scope changes, compliance validations, and stakeholder commitments to the human Project Manager.
